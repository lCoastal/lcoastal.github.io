Best Luck Cap

Every mineral, every zone it drops in, and the luck cap that finds it most often there.

Heads up: this uses the same luck model as Luck Odds, with the luck you type in. A cap only does something when it's below your luck (then you pan at the cap); no cap means you pan at your own luck. Drop chances come from the in-game zone weights, but how luck turns into rolls is still a model, so treat the best cap as a strong hint rather than a guarantee. Abyssal Depth only shows up for minerals that drop nowhere else.

How this is worked out

  • More luck pulls rarer minerals into each roll, which also pushes less rare ones out. So each mineral has a luck where it shows up most often, and going past it makes it rarer again.
  • For every zone, each cap setting below your luck (1 up to 100,000) is tried against no cap, and the one that gives that mineral the best chance is shown. Caps at or above your luck do nothing, so they count as no cap.
  • "Best luck" is the exact luck that finds it most often there, if you could set any number. The caps only come in fixed steps, so this shows where the real peak sits between them.
  • "Compared to your luck" is how much more often you'd find it with that cap than panning uncapped at your luck.
  • The rarest minerals in a zone only get better with more luck, so for those, no cap is best.

The formula

For one zone, list its minerals from rarest to most common, with drop shares p₁, p₂, …, pₙ (as fractions that add up to 1). For the mineral in spot k:

Everything rarera = p₁ + p₂ + … + pₖ₋₁
Up to and including itb = a + pₖ
Best of L rollsf(L) = (1 − a)L − (1 − b)L
Per mineralP = ¼·f(1) + ¼·f(max(1, L ÷ 2)) + ½·f(max(1, L))
Per pan (capacity C)1 − (1 − P)√C
Pans on average1 ÷ (per pan chance)
With a luck capL = min(your luck, cap)
Quick estimateL₀ = ln( ln(1 − b) ÷ ln(1 − a) ) ÷ ln( (1 − a) ÷ (1 − b) )
Best luck (exact)the L where ⅛·g(L ÷ 2) + ½·g(L) = 0
whereg(x) = (1 − a)x·ln(1 − a) − (1 − b)x·ln(1 − b)

That equation has no neat closed form, so the tool solves it numerically. L₀ is where the full-luck part alone peaks; the real peak sits a bit above it, usually around 1.2 × L₀. The quarter of rolls at half luck are still climbing when the full-luck rolls peak, so they drag the peak to the right, but they only pull with ⅛ of the strength (a quarter of the rolls, moving half as fast) against the full-luck rolls' ½. For most minerals that lands at 1.18–1.25 × L₀; a mineral far more common than everything rarer than it can go up to about 1.5 × L₀. The quarter at luck 1 never changes, so it doesn't move the peak. The rarest mineral in a zone has a = 0, so it has no peak: higher luck is always better.
