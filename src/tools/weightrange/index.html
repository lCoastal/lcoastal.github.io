Weight Range

How heavy a mineral can come out with your Size Boost and capacity: the lightest, typical and heaviest weight, and how often each size shows up. Pick a zone instead to see every mineral there at once.

Heads up: the lightest and heaviest weights are exact. How often each weight shows up is fitted on about 1.5 million logged minerals at 1,856 to 7,240 capacity, so outside that range it's an estimate.

The formula

WeightW = B × (1 + S) × k × M
B, S, Mbase weight · Size Boost (1,476% → 14.76) · 1.5 with Solar Magnifier, 2 in a Volcanic Eruption zone
k (lightest – heaviest)0.75 ≤ k ≤ 15.5
k, small roll26.1% of minerals: k uniform from 0.75 to 1.88
k, heavy rollh of minerals: k = 0.75 + 14.75 · x, x ~ Beta(a, 1.99)
k, even rollthe rest (73.9% − h): k uniform from 0.75 to 15.5
h and a from capacity Ch = 0.533 · (1 − e−(C − 1797)/2266) (0 below 1,797) · a = 4.24 + 0.000667 · C
  • Lightest and heaviest: every logged mineral falls between 0.75 and 15.5 × base weight × (1 + Size Boost). Common minerals in big runs hit both ends almost exactly.
  • Capacity matters: about a quarter of minerals always come out small, near the lightest weight. The rest are spread evenly at low capacity, and the bigger your pan, the more of them lean towards the heavy end. The heaviest possible doesn't change.
  • What doesn't matter: luck, the modifier, which mineral it is and where in the pan it lands made no difference in the logs.
  • Solar Magnifier is ×1.5 on the final weight. Volcanic Eruption is ×2 size in its zones; whether it stacks with the Magnifier isn't tested, so the table shows it on its own.
