How It Works

How the game picks your minerals, modifiers and weights, as far as testing shows.
Change the numbers in each box to see what they do. These are the same models the rest of the toolkit uses.

THE LUCK FORMULA IS STILL AN ESTIMATE
It's fitted on testing, not the game's own formula. It matches most minerals closely, but some can still be off, especially the rarest ones. Treat the numbers here as a guide, not exact odds. Every test is in the Test Log.

Heads up: the game doesn't publish any of this. Everything here is worked out from testing and the wiki, so treat it as a very good guess, not the game's code. Some parts are still being tested, and the luck formula is not exact.

Luck: which mineral you get

Every zone has a drop table. With no luck, each mineral comes up at its listed chance. Luck works like extra rolls: the game rolls the table several times and keeps the rarest result. So luck doesn't add a flat bonus; it pushes every pick towards the rare end of the table, and common minerals get rarer as a result.

The best fit so far is that about 29% of minerals roll just once, about 3% roll roughly luck ÷ 23 times, about 19% roll about 0.4 × luck times and about 49% roll about 1.74 × luck times, keeping the rarest result. On top of that, minerals with base odds between about 1 in 18 million and 1 in 1.5 billion (most Mythics) come up about 1.86× more often than the rolls alone predict. That's why the gain slows down: going from 100 to 1,000 luck matters far more than 10,000 to 11,000 for anything that isn't very rare.

The wiki's Stat Systems Guide uses a simpler split (a quarter once, a quarter at half luck, half at full luck). Testing fits the split above much better, especially for mid-rarity minerals like Jade and Onyx and for very rare ones at high luck.

Ties and duplicates. Minerals with exactly the same listed odds share their chance evenly. The Duplicator trinket gives Mythic and Exotic ores a 10% chance to be duplicated, so with it you see a few more of those than the luck alone gives.

Luck caps give less than they say. A capped session behaves like about 56% of the cap: measured on 326,000 capped minerals in 17 zones, every cap from 100 to 10,000 landed between 54% and 60%. Best Luck Cap, Dredge, Build Compare and the farm routes use it.

Events change your luck too. A Meteor Shower doubles your final luck and weather events (Nimue's Blessing, Blizzard, Swamp Fog, Starfall) multiply it by 1.5, and they stack. Hallows' Eve, the Luck Totem and most other boosts add to your multiplier instead. Autopan without the Mastered enchant digs at 51% quality, which gives about two thirds of your luck. Best Luck Cap, Dredge and Luck Odds have a setting for events and autopan.

The Senet Die rolls once per full pan and multiplies all of your luck for that pan, events included: ×0.5, ×0.75, ×1, ×1.25, ×1.5 or ×3, each equally likely. On average that's ×1.33.

For a mineral that's the rarest in its zone. Something with rarer minerals above it gains less, because those take the rare rolls first.

Capacity: how many minerals per pan

A full pan doesn't give one mineral per point of capacity. On average it gives about 0.68 × capacity0.565 minerals, so 400 capacity is about 20, 1,600 is about 44, 5,462 about 87 and 7,240 about 102. Every pan lands between ½ × √capacity and about 2.5 × √capacity, and most pans sit in the lower two thirds of that range, which is why the average is below the middle. Doubling capacity gives roughly 1.4× the minerals, so capacity helps steadily but never explodes. Luck doesn't change the count. The Pan Size tool shows yours.

What the next +1,000 capacity is worth

Because of the 0.565 power, each extra 1,000 capacity adds less than the one before. The line shows how many more minerals per pan (and so how much better your odds at any one mineral) the next +1,000 gives at each capacity. Luck doesn't fade like this for the rarest minerals: +1% luck stays about +1% odds, so the more capacity you have, the more luck a point of capacity is worth giving up. The dot is the capacity you typed in.

Worked out as 1 capacity ≈ 0.565 × luck ÷ capacity in luck points, for a mineral that's the rarest in its zone (where odds go up in step with luck). For less rare minerals luck is worth less, so capacity is worth even more. This is per pan, not per hour: bigger pans take longer.

Modifiers: how often and which one

How many minerals get a normal modifier rises with Modifier Boost only (luck doesn't change it): about 1 − 0.937boost (×), so roughly 7% at 113%, 32% at 600%, 45% at 926% and 66% at 1,660%. Modifier Boost also changes which modifier you get. It works like luck does for minerals: the game rolls the modifier several times by its weight and keeps the rarest. The roll strength is about 11.7 × boost (×)0.84 + 2 per 1,000 luck (no cap), with 31% of rolls once, 13% at about a sixth of the strength and 56% at full strength. A Modifier Surge doubles the boost part.

Special modifiers come from their own sources and sit on top of that: Treasured only from treasure chests (non-Exotic ores of that chest's zone) and the Pharaoh's Tomb deposits, Entombed only from the tomb deposits, Voidtorn from Void tools or The Void, Electrified from Thunderstorms and Crystalline from geodes or Crystal Touch. Feather of Ma'at always comes Treasured.

At very high Modifier Boost the formula keeps climbing (about 80% at 2,500%), but that end is less certain.

Weight: how heavy a mineral comes out

A mineral's weight is base weight × (1 + Size Boost) × a roll between 0.75 and 15.5, with Size Boost as a number (1,476% is 14.76). So the lightest is about 0.75 × base × (1 + Size Boost) and the heaviest about 15.5 × base × (1 + Size Boost). Both are best guesses from the lowest and highest rolls in our test logs, not confirmed limits. The Solar Magnifier multiplies the final weight by 1.5, and a Volcanic Eruption doubles sizes in its zones.

The zone matters too. Every zone multiplies weights by its own factor, from ×0.80 in Rubble Creek to ×1.20 in Timelocked Sanctuary; most zones are within a few percent of ×1. Pick the zone below to include it.

The roll isn't spread evenly. About a quarter of minerals always roll small, right near the bottom. The rest spread across the whole range, evenly at low capacity, and the bigger your pan, the more of them lean towards the heavy end. The heaviest possible doesn't change with capacity, only how often you get close to it. Luck, the modifier and which mineral it is make no difference.

Weight Range shows the full spread for any mineral, and Size Boost Finder works backwards from the weight you want.

Museum: how boosts add up

Every display adds its mineral's boost (at or above its max-boost weight) plus its modifier. All of it for one stat is added together, then multiplies that stat: a total of +2.5 Luck means ×3.5 Luck.

A modifier adds the same amount to every stat it gives, by the display's rarity: Common 0.005, Uncommon 0.0075, Rare 0.0125, Epic 0.02, Legendary 0.03, Mythic 0.05, Exotic 0.08. Perfect gives Luck, Capacity and Size Boost; Mutated gives Luck, Dig Strength and Modifier Boost; Treasured and Entombed give double, on Luck only. Each rarity has its own displays (Exotic 1, Mythic 2, the rest 3), and a mineral can only be in one of them.

Museum Planner and Museum Improver use exactly this.
