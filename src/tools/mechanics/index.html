How It Works

How the game picks your minerals, modifiers and weights, as far as my own tests show.
Change the numbers in each box to see what they do. These are the same models the rest of the toolkit uses.

Heads up: the game doesn't publish any of this. Everything here is worked out from my tests and the wiki, so treat it as a very good guess, not the game's code. Weight is the least certain part and is still being tested.

Luck: which mineral you get

Every zone has a drop table. With no luck, each mineral comes up at its listed chance. Luck works like extra rolls: the game rolls the table several times and keeps the rarest result. So luck doesn't add a flat bonus; it pushes every pick towards the rare end of the table, and common minerals get rarer as a result.

The best fit is that a quarter of minerals roll once, a quarter roll luck ÷ 2 times and half roll luck times. That's why the gain slows down: going from 100 to 1,000 luck matters far more than 10,000 to 11,000 for anything that isn't very rare.

For a mineral that's the rarest in its zone. Something with rarer minerals above it gains less, because those take the rare rolls first.

Capacity: how many tries per pan

A full pan doesn't give one roll per slot. It behaves like √capacity separate picks, so 400 capacity is about 20 picks and 1,600 is about 40. Doubling capacity gives roughly 1.4× the picks, which is why capacity helps steadily but never explodes.

Modifiers: how often and which one

About 36% of panned minerals get a normal modifier, and Modifier Boost barely changes that. What it changes is which modifier you get. It works like luck does for minerals: the game rolls the modifier several times by its weight and keeps the rarest. The roll strength is about 10.1 per 100% Modifier Boost + 1 per 1,000 luck, with a third of rolls once, a quarter at half strength and the rest at full strength.

Voidtorn, Treasured, Electrified and other event modifiers come from their own sources and sit on top of the 36%.

Weight: what's known so far

  • Every mineral has a base weight (shown on its Minerals page), and its weights scale with it: a mineral with twice the base weight comes out about twice as heavy.
  • On top of that there's a random roll, so the same mineral can land anywhere in a fairly wide range. A good share of minerals get an extra bonus on top of that roll, which is where the heavy ones come from.
  • Luck doesn't change weight at all in my tests. Capacity does make very heavy finds more common, because more picks means more chances at the top of the range.
  • Size Boost raises weight, but not as a straight multiplier: doubling it doesn't double your weights. The exact curve still needs more testing.
