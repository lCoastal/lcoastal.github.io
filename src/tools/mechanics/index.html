How It Works

How the game picks your minerals, modifiers and weights, as far as my own tests show.
Change the numbers in each box to see what they do. These are the same models the rest of the toolkit uses.

⚠ THE LUCK FORMULA IS NOT CORRECT
It was updated on 11/11/26 to fit my latest tests better, but it's still not the game's real formula. Odds, best caps and best zones here are a rough guide only, and can be clearly wrong, especially at very high luck. I'm still testing it.

Heads up: the game doesn't publish any of this. Everything here is worked out from my tests and the wiki, so treat it as a very good guess, not the game's code. Some parts are still being tested, and the luck formula is not exact.

Luck: which mineral you get

Every zone has a drop table. With no luck, each mineral comes up at its listed chance. Luck works like extra rolls: the game rolls the table several times and keeps the rarest result. So luck doesn't add a flat bonus; it pushes every pick towards the rare end of the table, and common minerals get rarer as a result.

My best fit so far is that about 30% of minerals roll just once, about 9% roll a much weaker number of times (roughly luck ÷ 8.5) and about 61% roll a bit more than your full luck. That's why the gain slows down: going from 100 to 1,000 luck matters far more than 10,000 to 11,000 for anything that isn't very rare.

The wiki's Stat Systems Guide uses a simpler split (a quarter once, a quarter at half luck, half at full luck). My tests, with over half a million minerals, fit the split above much better, especially for mid-rarity minerals like Jade and Onyx.

Events change your luck too. A Meteor Shower doubles your final luck and weather events (Nimue's Blessing, Blizzard, Swamp Fog, Starfall) multiply it by 1.5, and they stack. Hallows' Eve, the Luck Totem and most other boosts add to your multiplier instead. Autopan without the Mastered enchant digs at 51% quality, which gives about two thirds of your luck. Best Luck Cap, Dredge and Luck Odds have a setting for events and autopan.

For a mineral that's the rarest in its zone. Something with rarer minerals above it gains less, because those take the rare rolls first.

Capacity: how many tries per pan

A full pan doesn't give one roll per slot. It behaves like √capacity separate picks, so 400 capacity is about 20 picks and 1,600 is about 40. Doubling capacity gives roughly 1.4× the picks, which is why capacity helps steadily but never explodes.

Modifiers: how often and which one

About 36% of panned minerals get a normal modifier, and Modifier Boost barely changes that. What it changes is which modifier you get. It works like luck does for minerals: the game rolls the modifier several times by its weight and keeps the rarest. The roll strength is about 10.1 per 100% Modifier Boost + 1 per 1,000 luck (the luck part stops at 50), with a third of rolls once, a quarter at half strength and the rest at full strength.

Voidtorn, Treasured, Electrified and other event modifiers come from their own sources and sit on top of the 36%.

The 36% held steady in my tests between 600% and 920% Modifier Boost. The wiki says it climbs towards 99% near 1,900% effective boost, which I haven't been able to test yet.

Weight: what's known so far

  • Every mineral has a base weight (shown on its Minerals page), and its weights scale with it: a mineral with twice the base weight comes out about twice as heavy.
  • On top of that there's a random roll, so the same mineral can land anywhere in a fairly wide range. A good share of minerals get an extra bonus on top of that roll, which is where the heavy ones come from.
  • Luck doesn't change weight at all in my tests. Capacity raises weights slowly: going from about 1,900 to 5,300 capacity made minerals about 1.25× heavier on average.
  • Size Boost is a straight multiplier: double it and your weights double. My tests and the wiki agree on this.
